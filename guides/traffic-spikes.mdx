Every plan has a monthly request quota — see the [FAQ](/faq) for current limits. This protects you from runaway traffic consuming your monthly allowance unexpectedly.

## How quotas work

Free is a hard cap: once you hit it, further requests are blocked until the cycle resets or you upgrade. Paid plans allow metered overage past the included quota, up to a ceiling, before anything is blocked — see the [FAQ](/faq) for exactly how overage works.

Quota is enforced before your destination is ever called — a request that's over the limit never reaches your backend.

---

## What happens at the limit

When a request would put you over your plan's limit, getrequest returns:

```
HTTP 429 Too Many Requests
```

The request never reaches your backend — but it **is still logged**, tagged so you can tell a quota block apart from any other failure and identify exactly what was blocked once you upgrade or the quota resets.

<Note>
  Quota resets on your billing anniversary date, not on the 1st of each month.
</Note>

---

## Monitor your usage

Check your current consumption in **Settings → Usage**:

- **Requests used** — total for the current billing period
- **Requests remaining** — how much quota is left
- **Daily breakdown** — a chart of request volume by day so you can spot growth trends early

Use this to predict when you'll hit your limit before it happens.

---

## Upgrade before you hit the wall

If you're approaching your quota, upgrade your plan from **Settings → Subscription**. The new quota takes effect immediately — mid-period upgrades are prorated.

<Tip>
  Watch for a spike in daily volume that's 2–3× your normal baseline. That's usually a sign of a retry loop, a misconfigured client, or a load test hitting your endpoints.
</Tip>

---

## Per-endpoint traffic visibility

The Logs view shows every request by endpoint slug. If a spike is happening, you can filter by endpoint to identify which one is driving the volume — and whether it's legitimate traffic or noise.

Use the **method** and **status** filters together to find automated retries: a flood of identical POST requests with 4xx responses is a common pattern.

---

## Rate limits on dashboard actions

In addition to request quotas, a few dashboard actions have their own rate limits:

| Action | Limit |
|--------|-------|
| Shareable link generation | 30 per minute |
| Request replay | 20 per minute |
| Bulk retry (manual selection) | 10 per minute |
| Bulk retry job preview count | 30 per minute |
| Bulk retry job start | 5 per minute |
| Bulk retry job cancel | 10 per minute |

These are per-user limits and reset automatically. See [Bulk retry & recovering many requests](/guides/bulk-retry) for what each of these actions does.

Between the quota, the usage dashboard, and per-endpoint filtering, you should never be surprised by a spike after the fact — you'll see it building in **Settings → Usage** before it ever turns into a 429.
