title: Handle backend downtime
description: Understand what GetRequest captures during an outage and how to pick up where you left off.
---

When your backend goes down, requests don't disappear — GetRequest captures them. You have a complete record of everything that came in while your backend was unavailable.

## What happens during an outage

With a **Forward** endpoint, GetRequest attempts to proxy every incoming request to your upstream. If your backend is unreachable or returns an error:

1. GetRequest captures the full incoming request (headers, body, method, path).
2. GetRequest records the upstream response — including `5xx` errors, timeouts, and connection failures.
3. The caller receives the upstream error status directly.
4. The log entry is saved with the failure details.

Nothing is silently dropped. Every request that reached GetRequest during the outage is in your Logs.

---

## The 3-second timeout

Forward endpoints wait up to **3 seconds** for your upstream to respond. If your backend takes longer — or doesn't respond at all — GetRequest returns:

```
HTTP 504 Gateway Timeout
```

The full request is still logged. Use the log entry to replay it once your backend recovers.

<Note>
  The 3-second limit applies to the upstream connection and response. GetRequest's own overhead is well under 5ms.
</Note>

---

## Identify what failed

Open **Logs** and filter by status code to find everything that failed during the incident:

1. Set the **Status** filter to `5xx` or `504`.
2. Set the **Time range** to the incident window.
3. Click any row to see the full request — what the caller sent, what your backend returned (or failed to return), and how long it took.

This gives you an exact record of impact: how many requests failed, which endpoints were affected, and the precise time window.

---

## What to do next

Once your backend is back up:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Confirm recovery">
    Send a test request to your endpoint and verify you get a `2xx` response.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Find failed requests">
    Filter Logs by `5xx` and `504` for the incident window.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Replay each one">
    Open each failed log entry and click **Replay** (Pro). GetRequest re-fires the exact request to your backend.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Verify the response">
    Confirm each replay returns `2xx`. The new response is shown immediately in the log detail.
  </Step>
</Steps>

See [Recover from production failures](/guides/recover-failures) for a detailed replay walkthrough.

---

## Switching to JSON mode during maintenance

If you're taking your backend down intentionally (planned maintenance, deploy), switch affected endpoints to `JSON` mode first:

1. Edit the endpoint and change **Action** to `JSON`.
2. Set a meaningful response body — a maintenance message or a cached last-known-good response.
3. Callers receive the JSON immediately instead of a 504.
4. All requests are still logged.
5. When your backend is back, switch back to `Forward` and replay anything that came in.

This eliminates 504s from planned downtime entirely.
